MUMBAI URBAN GROWTH & SPATIAL INEQUALITY MODELING SYSTEM
Comprehensive System Architecture, Model Formulations, and Execution Walkthrough
================================================================================

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW & ARCHITECTURAL OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project provides an end-to-end geospatial analytical pipeline designed to:
  a. Ingest multi-decadal satellite observation data across the Mumbai Metropolitan
     Region (MMR) and its 24 Municipal Administrative Wards (BMC).
  b. Quantify spatial disparities between green space access (ecological health)
     and nighttime light radiance (socio-economic activity).
  c. Train and calibrate an urban growth transition model based on 20 years of
     empirical satellite observations (2000–2020).
  d. Forecast urban built-up expansion, saturation limits, and environmental
     decay up to 2035 across all 24 administrative wards.


================================================================================
2. DETAILED WORKING & PURPOSE OF EACH MODEL
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODEL 1: Remote Sensing Feature Extraction & Multispectral Indexing Model
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Location: src/ee_pipeline.py
• Purpose:
  Extracts biophysical and socioeconomic indicator layers from raw multispectral
  and radiometric satellite imagery.

• Working Principles & Mathematics:
  1. Copernicus Sentinel-2 Cloud-Masked Pre-Monsoon Composite:
     - Filtered between January 1 and April 30 (the pre-monsoon dry season in
       Mumbai ensures minimal cloud interference and cloud-shadow distortion).
     - Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI):
         NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red)  ==>  (B8 - B4) / (B8 + B4)
         Range: -1.0 to +1.0. High values (>0.35) indicate dense vegetation canopy;
         low values (<0.18) denote paved, impervious built-up environments with
         high heat-island vulnerability.
     - Normalized Difference Built-up Index (NDBI):
         NDBI = (SWIR1 - NIR) / (SWIR1 + NIR) ==> (B11 - B8) / (B11 + B8)
         Highlights concrete, asphalt, and built structures.

  2. NOAA VIIRS Day/Night Band (Nighttime Lights - NTL):
     - Sensor: Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS).
     - Metric: Average Radiance (nW/cm²/sr).
     - Purpose: Serves as a reliable, objective proxy for electrification,
       commercial intensity, and spatial economic concentration.

  3. Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL - Built Surface Grid):
     - Source: European Commission JRC (P2023A collection).
     - Band: 'built_surface' (m² of built structure footprint per 100m grid cell).
     - Multi-temporal epochs: 2000, 2010, 2020.
     - Provides the ground truth physical expansion baseline.

  4. WorldPop High-Resolution Gridded Population:
     - Statistical downscaling of national census records allocating estimated
       population counts per 100m pixel.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODEL 2: Cloud-Side Zonal Reduction & Spatial Inequality Aggregation Engine
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Location: src/spatial_inequality.py
• Purpose:
  Aggregates continuous 100m satellite pixels into discrete administrative
  governance units (the 24 BMC Administrative Wards: Ward A to Ward T).

• Working Principles:
  1. Vector-Raster Spatial Intersect:
     Loads the official Mumbai BMC 24 Ward polygons and sends them to Google's
     cloud server infrastructure via the Earth Engine API.
  2. Cloud Zonal Statistics:
     Uses ee.Reducer.mean() at a scale of 100 meters to integrate all multiband
     pixels within the geometric boundary of each ward:
       Mean_Metric(Ward_i) = (1 / Area) * ∫∫ Pixel_Value(x, y) dx dy
  3. Derived Spatial Metrics:
     - 20-Year Built-up Growth Rate (%):
         Growth% = [(Built_2020 - Built_2000) / Built_2000] * 100
     - Per-Capita Economic Radiance Proxy:
         Econ_Proxy = [Nighttime_Lights / (Population_Density + 1)] * 1000


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODEL 3: Spatial Inequality Indexing & Lorenz Distribution Formulation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Location: src/urban_growth_model.py
• Purpose:
  Measures the macroeconomic and ecological disparity across Mumbai's wards.

• Working Principles & Mathematics:
  1. Gini Coefficient Calculation:
     Calculates the Gini inequality coefficient across the sorted distribution of
     ward metrics:
       G = [2 * Σ (i * y_i)] / [n * Σ y_i] - (n + 1) / n
     Where:
       - y_i is the ranked attribute value of ward i
       - n is the total number of administrative wards (n = 24)
       - G = 0 represents perfect equality; G = 1 represents total concentration.

  2. Inequality Metrics Computed for Mumbai:
     - Green Canopy Disparity Gini   : 0.1823 (stark divergence in tree cover)
     - Economic Radiance Gini        : 0.2026 (high capital clustering in BKC/South)
     - Built-up Spatial Gini         : 0.2018 (island city saturation vs suburbs)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODEL 4: Logistic Urban Growth Transition & Saturation Forecasting Model
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Location: src/urban_growth_model.py
• Purpose:
  Calibrates an empirical urban expansion transition curve from historical
  epochs and forecasts spatial built-up footprint up to 2035.

• Working Principles & Mathematics:
  1. Governing Non-linear Differential Equation:
     Urban horizontal land conversion cannot expand infinitely. It is bounded by
     physical geography (Arabian Sea, Sanjay Gandhi National Park, mangroves) and
     reaches an asymptotic carrying capacity K:
       dB/dt = r * B(t) * [1 - B(t) / K]

  2. Calibration from Empirical Satellite Epochs:
     - B(t) = Built-up surface footprint (m² per 100m cell).
     - Intrinsic Growth Rate (r):
       Derived using continuous compound growth from the 2000–2020 observations:
         r ≈ [ln(B_2020) - ln(B_2000)] / 20
     - Carrying Capacity (K):
       In Mumbai, the peak empirical density observed is ~4,000 m² per 100m grid
       (achieved by Ward C - Marine Lines/Kalbadevi).
       - Saturated Island Wards: K = min(4200, B_2020 * 1.08)
       - Established Suburbs   : K = min(3600, B_2020 * 1.25)
       - Outer Expanding Suburbs: K = min(3000, max(B_2020 * 1.45, 1800))

  3. Numerical Simulation:
     Applies Euler's step simulation with dt = 1.0 year across:
       - 2020 to 2025 (5 steps) -> Validated against early 2025 baseline
       - 2025 to 2030 (5 steps) -> Medium-term expansion target
       - 2030 to 2035 (5 steps) -> Long-term saturation projection

  4. Environmental Feedback (NDVI Decay):
     As the built-up area approaches saturation, natural permeable land is paved:
       NDVI_2030 = max(0.08, NDVI_current * [1 - 0.4 * (ΔB / K)])

4. CONCRETE DEMO EXAMPLE: CONTRASTING TWO MUMBAI WARDS
================================================================================

To see exactly how the formulas and models compute real numbers, examine this
side-by-side comparison between an Expanding Outer Suburb and a Saturated Core:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE A: WARD P/N (Malad, Marve, Madh Island — Expanding Suburb)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Satellite Raw Observations Extracted:
   • 2000 Built-Up Surface : 1,009.63 m² / 100m cell
   • 2010 Built-Up Surface : 1,109.79 m² / 100m cell
   • 2020 Built-Up Surface : 1,169.03 m² / 100m cell
   • 20-Year Growth Rate   : +15.79% (One of the fastest-growing wards in Mumbai)
   • Vegetation (NDVI)     : 0.3482 (High green buffer from mangroves & Madh)
   • Nighttime Lights (NTL): 16.63 nW/cm²/sr (Moderate residential illumination)

2. Growth Model Parameter Calibration:
   • Intrinsic Rate (r)    : ln(1169.03 / 1009.63) / 20 = 0.00732 (0.73% per yr)
   • Carrying Capacity (K) : 1,800.00 m² (Limited by coastal regulation & creeks)
   • Saturation in 2020    : (1,169.03 / 1,800.00) * 100 = 64.9% (Ample expansion room)

3. Forecast Projections:
   • 2025 Simulated Built-up: 1,184.22 m²
   • 2030 Simulated Built-up: 1,199.04 m²
   • 2035 Simulated Built-up: 1,213.36 m² (Delta: +44.33 m² additional built area)
   • Projected 2030 NDVI   : 0.3448 (Slight loss of vegetation as outer parcels develop)
   • Classification        : "Moderate Growth Corridor"


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE B: WARD C (Bhuleshwar, Marine Lines, Kalbadevi — Saturated Historic Core)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Satellite Raw Observations Extracted:
   • 2000 Built-Up Surface : 3,856.23 m² / 100m cell
   • 2010 Built-Up Surface : 3,857.44 m² / 100m cell
   • 2020 Built-Up Surface : 3,863.51 m² / 100m cell
   • 20-Year Growth Rate   : +0.19% (Virtually zero horizontal expansion)
   • Vegetation (NDVI)     : 0.1213 (Lowest green cover in the entire city)
   • Nighttime Lights (NTL): 50.72 nW/cm²/sr (Highest commercial economic density)

2. Growth Model Parameter Calibration:
   • Intrinsic Rate (r)    : ln(3863.51 / 3856.23) / 20 = 0.000094 (Near zero)
   • Carrying Capacity (K) : 4,172.59 m² (Already near absolute physical limit)
   • Saturation in 2020    : (3,863.51 / 4,172.59) * 100 = 92.6% (Fully Saturated)

3. Forecast Projections:
   • 2025 Simulated Built-up: 3,864.88 m²
   • 2030 Simulated Built-up: 3,866.25 m²
   • 2035 Simulated Built-up: 3,867.62 m² (Delta: +4.11 m²; near static)
   • Projected 2030 NDVI   : 0.1213 (Remains flat; almost no vegetation left to lose)
   • Classification        : "Saturated / Infill Only"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY CONCLUSION OF THE COMPARISON:
The model mathematically captures Mumbai's dual urban dynamic:
The Island City (Ward C) is locked in horizontal saturation and suffers from
severe green space deprivation (NDVI 0.121) despite high economic light radiance
(50.72 nW). In contrast, the outer suburbs (Ward P/N) retain a greener canopy
(NDVI 0.348) and are absorbing over 10x more horizontal expansion pressure (+44.3 m²
vs +4.1 m²) through 2035.
